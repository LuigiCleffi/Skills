# Step 2. Form a Positioning Team

Positioning is a business strategy decision, so it must be driven by the person who owns the business of that product and worked out by a small cross-functional team whose functions will later execute it. A positioning exercise that is not a team effort led by the business leader will not be adopted. Use the first session to surface the different assumptions each group holds about attributes, value and target markets, then align everyone on one position.

## Who leads

- The person responsible for the product's business must be in the room and be seen as driving the effort: in a startup the CEO and/or founders; in a larger company the division or business-unit leader, occasionally the head of marketing or product.
- Marketing often feels the pain of weak positioning first and starts the conversation, but it cannot "own" positioning any more than it can own the business strategy. It is too broad to live in one silo.

## Why every function is involved

Positioning feeds outputs across the company:

| Function | Positioning drives | Perspective it brings |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | Messaging, audience targeting, campaigns | How prospects respond early in the journey |
| Sales and business development | Target segments, account strategy | Who buys, objections, what rivals come up in deals |
| Customer success | Onboarding, expansion strategy | Who succeeds and stays, who struggles |
| Product and development | Roadmap and prioritization | Real technical differentiators; what is and is not possible |

- Functions meet customers at different points of the buying journey, so they hold different views of competitive alternatives, unique features and best-fit customers. Those differences are the raw material.
- You need engagement from every group because you need buy-in from every group: the leaders who will execute should help build it.

## Size and composition

- One, at most two, senior people per function.
- Too many people and some voices disappear or one group dominates. Sessions work with as few as three and as many as about twelve.

## Facilitation

- Dunford strongly recommends an experienced outside facilitator. Internal attempts often stall on strategic disagreements and on assumptions nobody feels free to question.
- The facilitator's job: make sure every voice is heard, gently challenge "untouchable" assumptions, and keep the group moving through the steps in order.
- If no outsider is available, pick a facilitator with no stake in the current positioning and give them explicit permission to challenge the CEO.

## Worked example

For the HVAC dispatch SaaS: sponsor is the CEO; attendees are the VP marketing, a senior account executive and the head of sales, the customer-success manager for top accounts, and the head of product; facilitator is an outside positioning consultant. In the first hour sales describes the product as "scheduling software", product calls it "a field-service platform" and success says best customers use it "to answer emergency calls faster". That gap is exactly what the session must resolve.

## Workflow

1. Identify the business owner and confirm they will sponsor and attend.
2. Pick one or two senior people from marketing, sales, customer success and product; keep the group at roughly three to twelve.
3. Arrange a neutral, preferably external, facilitator.
4. Circulate pre-work so discussion starts from evidence.
5. Open by asking each function to state, independently, the product's alternatives, differentiators and best customers; record the disagreements.
6. Agree on the goal: one position the whole company understands and supports. Then move to [Step 3](../PositioningBaggage/positioningBaggage.md).

## Red flags

- "Marketing owns positioning" or a positioning project run by a junior marketer.
- The CEO or business owner delegates and skips the sessions.
- Product, sales or success not represented, then refusing to adopt the result.
- A crowd of twenty where one department does all the talking.
- An internal facilitator who is also the most invested defender of the current position.
- The output is announced to sales rather than built with them.
