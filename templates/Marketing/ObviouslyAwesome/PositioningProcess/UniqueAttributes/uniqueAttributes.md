# Step 5. Isolate Your Unique Attributes or Features

Strong positioning is centered on what the product does best. With the competitive alternatives in hand, list every capability you have that those alternatives lack, stay at the level of features (what the product or company *has* or *does*), and require proof for anything that is really a claim. Do not yet decide what matters or translate into value; that is Step 6.

## Attributes, not value

- A feature or attribute is something your product or company has or does: "15-megapixel camera", "integrates with QuickBooks", "one-click install", "metal construction".
- Keep this list free of benefits and value statements; mixing them now blurs the next step.

## List broadly

- Compare against the alternative groups from [Step 4](../CompetitiveAlternatives/competitiveAlternatives.md): what can you do that they cannot?
- Include everything, even items whose value is unclear or that some colleagues see as negative. Dunford's workshop example: the only vendor requiring an onsite professional-services install. Sales saw slower time to value; customer service saw a unique high-touch, customizable offer some customers wanted.
- Mine customer answers to "why did you choose us?".
- Look beyond product features:
  - Patented or proprietary technology or process.
  - Specialized expertise or a unique combination of skills and experience (the typical source for consultancies, agencies and dev shops).
  - Experience with a particular kind of project or client.
  - Partnerships, integrations, distribution channels.
  - Sourcing and production (the only hat maker sourcing a certain material; makeup from your own organic garden).
  - Delivery model (SaaS vs on-premise) and business model (leasing instead of selling).

## Your opinion is not proof

- The most common pseudo-attributes are "outstanding customer service" and "very easy to use". No company thinks its service is bad, and ease of use is really a value, not a feature.
- Turn each claim into the provable characteristic behind it:

| Claim | Ask | Provable attribute |
|---|---|---|
| "Great customer service" | How would we prove it? | More support staff per customer; certified agents; 24/7 phone coverage with measured response time |
| "Easy to use" | What makes it easier, and compared with what? | No training required where alternatives need it; measured time to proficiency |
| "Fast" | Faster than which alternative, at what? | Benchmarked results under stated conditions |

- Proof that counts: objective, measurable facts; independent reviewers or analysts saying it; an approved customer quote comparing you favorably with an alternative.

## Consideration versus retention attributes

- **Consideration attributes** matter when deciding whether to buy; they connect to a goal the customer wants to achieve now.
- **Retention attributes** matter at renewal: being easy to do business with, quality of support.
- Positioning should concentrate on consideration attributes. Retention matters (misled buyers churn), but prospects who see no reason to consider you will never experience your excellent support.

## Template: attribute inventory

| Attribute (has / does) | Alternative group(s) that lack it | Proof available | Consideration or retention | Possibly negative for some? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Capture the long list now; grouping and pruning happen in [Step 6](../ValueThemes/valueThemes.md).

## Worked example

HVAC dispatch SaaS versus whiteboard dispatch, big field-service suites and answering services:

| Attribute | Lacking in | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Routes an incoming emergency call to the nearest qualified, available tech automatically | Whiteboard, answering service, suites (manual assignment) | Product demo; median routing 40 s across 14 best customers |
| Native after-hours call answering with job-type triage | Whiteboard, suites | Feature; customer quote |
| Live tech location and skill tags | Whiteboard, answering service | Feature |
| Mobile app usable offline in basements | Some suites | Field test log |
| No invoicing or inventory module | (suites have them) | Listed even though sales sees it as a gap |
| "Easy to use" | — | Rewritten as: technicians productive after a 20-minute onboarding vs 2-day training for suites (customer data) |

## Workflow

1. Lay out the ranked alternative groups from Step 4 as columns.
2. Brainstorm every capability you have that each group lacks, across product, process, expertise, partnerships, channel, delivery and business model.
3. Add what best customers say made them choose you.
4. Keep controversial or "negative" attributes on the list, with a note.
5. Challenge every claim-like entry: convert to a provable fact or mark it unproven.
6. Tag each as consideration or retention; plan to lead with consideration attributes.
7. Do not prioritize yet; pass the list to value mapping.

## Red flags

- Differentiators that are adjectives: "best-in-class", "powerful", "seamless", "world-class support".
- Attributes that rivals have too, or that are unique only versus a competitor customers never consider.
- Value and features mixed in one list.
- A distinctive model trait (onsite install, human service, niche expertise) hidden because one team thinks it is a weakness.
- Retention traits (support quality, account management) as the lead differentiator in acquisition campaigns.
- Proof consisting only of the company's own assertions.
