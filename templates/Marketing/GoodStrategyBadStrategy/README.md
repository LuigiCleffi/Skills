## AI Skills

Before writing or reviewing a strategy, a go-to-market or marketing plan,
annual objectives or OKRs, a competitive response, or a growth or
acquisition rationale, find the skills that apply to the task in the
tables below and read them.

Do not read every skill file by default. Read only the skills whose
"Use when" matches the task, and follow their references to other skills
only when needed. Start with the kernel (chapter 5) for any strategy
document; the other chapters sharpen one part of it.

Each skill comes from one chapter of Richard Rumelt's *Good Strategy Bad
Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters* (Crown Business, 2011). The
skills frame each chapter for marketing, go-to-market, and business
strategy work. The book's case studies end around 2010; the skills carry a
short "Current note" where that matters.

# Context for AI: Strategy

## 1. Strategy Has a Kernel

Every strategy needs a diagnosis of the critical challenge, a guiding
policy for dealing with it, and coherent actions that carry out the
policy. Goals, visions, and targets are not strategy.

## 2. Name the Bad Strategy

Call out fluff, failure to face the challenge, goals mistaken for
strategy, and objectives that don't address the challenge. Rewrite them
into a kernel rather than polishing the wording.

## 3. Concentrate

Good strategy chooses where to apply force and what not to do. A plan that
funds every initiative equally has no strategy.

## 4. Make Objectives Proximate

Translate the guiding policy into near-term objectives the team can
actually achieve and measure.

## 5. Treat Strategy as a Hypothesis

State what you expect to happen and how you'll know you were wrong; test
small before committing big.
