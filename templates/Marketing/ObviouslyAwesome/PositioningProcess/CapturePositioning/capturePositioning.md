# Step 10. Capture Your Positioning so It Can Be Shared

Positioning is useless until the organization adopts it to drive branding, campaigns, sales strategy, product decisions and customer success. Capture it at three levels of detail: a full positioning document that breaks down each component and how they connect, a one-page version anyone can read, and a positioning canvas that shows the whole position at a glance. Do not reduce it to a positioning statement.

## Why not a statement

- A one-liner is too short to carry the subtleties of a position, and its contrived structure makes it hard to remember or repeat (see [five components](../../WhatIsPositioning/FiveComponents/fiveComponents.md)).

## The three artifacts

1. **Positioning document** — enough detail for marketing, sales and product to work from: each component explained, with evidence, and how the components relate (alternatives make attributes unique; attributes drive value; value defines who cares; together they justify the category).
2. **One-page summary** — concise, shareable across the company.
3. **Positioning canvas** — the high-level view, laid out so the relationships are visible.

## The positioning canvas

| Section | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Product name and one-line description | |
| Market category (and subcategory) | The macro market, and submarket if applicable, you compete in |

| Competitive alternatives | Unique attributes | Value | Who cares a lot |
|---|---|---|---|
| What would customers use if your product did not exist? | What features or capabilities do you have that the alternatives lack? | What value do those attributes enable for customers? | What characteristics make a customer care a lot about that value? |
| | | | |

Optional additions this skill recommends: a row for the relevant trend (Step 9), the positioning style chosen (Step 8), and a proof column next to value.

## Worked example: filled canvas

**Product**: DispatchNow — emergency dispatch software that gets the right technician to an urgent job first.

**Market category**: Field service management → emergency dispatch for residential HVAC and plumbing contractors. Style: big fish, small pond. Trend: heat-pump transition.

| Competitive alternatives | Unique attributes | Value | Who cares a lot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whiteboard plus phone and group text | Auto-routing of incoming emergency calls to nearest qualified tech | Win the emergency job before the caller dials the next contractor (median 40 s assignment) | Residential HVAC and plumbing contractors, 15 to 60 techs |
| All-in-one FSM suites | After-hours answering with job-type triage | Capture after-hours revenue without a night dispatcher | Advertise 24/7 service; run emergency-keyword ads |
| Answering service plus shared calendar | Live location and skill tags (including heat-pump certification); offline mobile app | Fewer wrong-tech callbacks; jobs closed on site | Emergency calls above 30 percent of revenue; no night dispatcher |

## Template: positioning document outline

1. Summary (the one-pager).
2. Best-fit customers: who they are, evidence, quotes.
3. Competitive alternatives: ranked groups, what customers like and dislike about each.
4. Unique attributes: list with proof.
5. Value themes: one to four, each with value statements and proof.
6. Who cares a lot: segment definition, identifiers, size versus goals, exclusions.
7. Market category and style: chosen frame, assumptions it triggers, what we must prove, risks.
8. Relevant trend (if any): link to value and proof.
9. Implications: sales story, messaging, roadmap, pricing, success (see [after positioning](../../PuttingPositioningIntoPlay/AfterPositioning/afterPositioning.md)).
10. Review date and triggers for revisiting.

## Workflow

1. Collect the outputs of Steps 1 to 9.
2. Fill the canvas first; check that every row reads across logically (alternative → attribute → value → who cares).
3. Write the full positioning document with evidence for each component.
4. Condense it into a one-page summary in plain language.
5. Have the business owner approve; circulate to every function with a walkthrough, not just an email.
6. Store it as the single source of truth that messaging, decks and briefs must reference.
7. Set a review date and record the triggers that would reopen it.

## Red flags

- The workshop ends with sticky notes and no written artifact.
- The output is a single positioning sentence.
- Canvas rows that do not connect (an attribute with no value, value with no segment that cares).
- Different teams holding different versions of the positioning.
- Agencies briefed from an old deck rather than the positioning document.
- No owner or review date.
