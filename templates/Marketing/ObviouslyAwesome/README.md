## AI Skills

Before positioning or repositioning a product or company, defining
best-fit customers, building competitor lists or battlecards, choosing a
market category, writing a positioning brief or messaging, or diagnosing
why prospects don't get the product, find the skills that apply to the
task in the tables below and read them.

Do not read every skill file by default. Read only the skills whose
"Use when" matches the task, and follow their references to other skills
only when needed. For a full positioning exercise, run the ten steps in
order; each step's output is the next step's input.

Each skill comes from one chapter or step of April Dunford's *Obviously
Awesome: How to Nail Product Positioning so Customers Get It, Buy It, Love
It* (Page Two, 2019). The steps share one invented running example so the
outputs connect from best customers to the finished canvas.

# Context for AI: Positioning

## 1. Positioning Is Context, Not Copy

Positioning decides what the product is, for whom, against what, and why
it matters. Messaging, taglines and campaigns come after it and are built
on it.

## 2. Start From the Best Customers

Derive positioning from the customers who get the most value, not from the
founders' intent or the widest possible market. Ask for real customer
evidence; don't invent it.

## 3. Compare Against Real Alternatives

Differentiation only means something against what customers would really
do instead, including spreadsheets, an intern, or doing nothing.

## 4. Choose a Frame That Makes Strengths Obvious

Pick the market category whose assumptions put the product's unique value
at the center, and say which strategy (head to head, subsegment, new
category) it implies.

## 5. Capture and Share

End with a filled positioning canvas the whole company can use, and state
which inputs were assumed rather than evidenced.
